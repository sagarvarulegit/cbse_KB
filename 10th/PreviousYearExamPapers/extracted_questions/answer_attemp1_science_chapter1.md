#### Q16. Difference between displacement and double displacement reaction
* **Student Answer:**  
  * **Displacement Reaction:** A chemical reaction in which a more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound.  
    *Example:* $\text{Fe}(s) + \text{CuSO}_4(aq) \rightarrow \text{FeSO}_4(aq) + \text{Cu}(s)$  
  * **Double Displacement Reaction:** A chemical reaction in which there is a mutual exchange of ions between two reactant compounds to form two new compounds.  
    *Example:* $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4(aq) + \text{BaCl}_2(aq) \rightarrow \text{BaSO}_4(s) + 2\text{NaCl}(aq)$
* **Reference from Platform Notes:**  
  > *From Revision Notes & Study Guide Sections 1.2.3 & 1.2.4:* Exact definitions and equations for displacement vs double displacement reactions.

#### Q21. Heating copper powder vs copper wares in air
* **Student Answer:**  
  * **Observation:** The reddish-brown copper powder turns into a black coating.

#### Q23. Decomposition definition, 3 equations & composting
* **Student Answer:**  
  (a) **Decomposition Reaction Definition:** A chemical reaction in which a single reactant breaks down into two or more simpler products when energy is supplied in the form of heat, light, or electricity.  
  * **Chemical Equations:**  
    1. **Heat (Thermal Decomposition):**  
       $$\text{CaCO}_3(s) \xrightarrow{\text{Heat}} \text{CaO}(s) + \text{CO}_2(g)$$  
    2. **Light (Photolytic Decomposition):**  
       $$2\text{AgCl}(s) \xrightarrow{\text{Sunlight}} 2\text{Ag}(s) + \text{Cl}_2(g)$$  
    3. **Electricity (Electrolytic Decomposition):**  
       $$2\text{H}_2\text{O}(l) \xrightarrow{\text{Electricity}} 2\text{H}_2(g) + \text{O}_2(g)$$  
  (b) **Composting Exothermic Nature:** The decomposition of vegetable matter into compost is considered exothermic because microorganisms break down complex organic molecules, releasing heat energy into the surroundings during the process.
* **Reference from Platform Notes:**  
  > *From Study Guide Section 1.2.2 (Decomposition Reactions) & Section 1.2.1 (Exothermic Reactions):* Complete definition, all 3 energy equations with state symbols, and composting reason.
